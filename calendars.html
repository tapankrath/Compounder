How setups are picked ›

Every option price is turned into an implied volatility. The real underlying price is read from the options (put-call parity), since a saved quote can be stale. The market's own price distribution at the near expiry is read from the near options (with the skew), so each option you sell is valued fairly rather than looking like free money.

The search tries every near and later expiry pair, short strikes from a quarter to one and a quarter of the expected move on each side, and for diagonals, long strikes 0.5%–2% further out. Each setup is valued at the near expiry, when the near options expire and the later ones are sold: once with volatility unchanged and once with it falling (at least as much as the market prices in for that period, more if you choose). Prices assume a fill about a third of the way from the mid toward the worst price.

Ranking is the average expected profit of the two cases divided by the worst loss. These are model estimates from today's prices, not forecasts; calendar and diagonal results depend heavily on how volatility moves, which no model knows.
